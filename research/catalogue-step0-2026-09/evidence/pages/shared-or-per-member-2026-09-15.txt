# Shared or per member

Every field and every relation our catalogue has today, sorted into what the family owns and what each sellable member owns. The fields are read off the real models in py/mono/solvent; the levels are now backed by a field-by-field survey of thirteen platforms, quoted and sourced, rather than by my opinion. Whether the two tables end up named Product → ProductVariant or ProductGroup → Product does not change this list: it is the same split either way.

## Images on both: yes, display can pick

The concern was the right one, and it is answered by two platforms that already carry images at both levels. The rule is one line; what needs care is that it has to be stated separately for each surface, which is the mistake Magento documents.

 The rule. images(member) = member's own images if it has any, otherwise the family's. The primary image is whichever of that resolved list has display_order = 0. Nothing merges the two lists, so there is never an ambiguous ordering between a family picture and a member picture.

This is precisely WooCommerce's shipped behaviour. Its variation falls back to the parent in the model — get_image_id() returns the parent's id when the variation has none — and the storefront "swaps the product gallery to that variation's images. Clearing the variation selection brings back the parent product gallery." Salesforce generalises the same idea: untagged image groups "represent the fallback images", and "the system chooses the most specific matching image groups possible".

### Magento's warning: one rule is not enough, name the surfaces

Magento decides this in three independent places, and ships them with different defaults — the detail gallery replaces the parent's images, the cart thumbnail falls back to the parent (<configurable_product_image>parent</configurable_product_image>), and swatches are a per-attribute flag. Left implicit, we would get the same drift. Ours, named:

Surface | What it shows | Where it lives today | 

Product page gallery | The family's images until the shopper picks a member, then that member's if it has any. Clearing the selection returns to the family's. | ts-product-detail-images | 

Card · search result | The resolved primary of whichever row the result is. Stays one cached string, as today. | Product.thumbnail_url_cache :441 | 

Merchant feed | Google requires image_link on every row, so each member resolves its own — its picture if it has one, else the family's. | products_api.py:243 | 

Basket · order line · label · POS | The member's resolved primary. These are about one physical unit, so they never show a family image the member has overridden. | basket :1164 · order :549 | 

### What it costs to build

One nullable column, one constraint. ProductImage.product becomes nullable and gains a sibling family FK, with a check that exactly one of the two is set. That keeps a single table and a single read path, which is what Shopify, Square and WooCommerce all do in their own way.

The feed query is the one real change. It is a single filter today — ProductImage.objects.filter(product_id__in=…, display_order=0) — and becomes two passes: members with their own primary, then the family's primary for the rest.

The thumbnail cache gains a fan-out. Editing a family image has to refresh thumbnail_url_cache on every member that was falling back to it. backfill_thumbnail_url_cache already walks this relationship and is the place to extend.

Nothing migrates. All 63,363 existing image rows stay attached to the member they are on now.

 The one thing this shape does not give you. Because the picture hangs off the member rather than off the axis value, a family that varies only by size will hold the same photograph once per member — five sizes, five uploads of the same bottle — or fall back to the family image and show nothing distinguishing. Every platform that solved this keyed the image to the varying option instead. If that duplication starts to hurt, the upgrade path is additive: the same nullable pointer, aimed at an axis value rather than at a member.

## Is it normal to carry a title on both rows?

Yes, but almost never as the same string twice. Where the family is a real container, the member's name is either absent, derived from its axis values, or a separate short label — and the displayed string is composed. Checked 2026-09-15 against the records and the vendor field tables.

Pattern | Platforms | What the source says | 

 Member has no name at all | 
 commercetools | 
 `name` is on ProductData only; the Modular projection's name is labelled "Name of the parent Product". | 

 Member name is derived and read-only | 
 Shopify · Square · WooCommerce | 
 Shopify: ProductVariant.title "has zero write paths" — variant title input fields across all 599 input objects = 0 — and displayName is "based on product's title + variant's title". Square: the variation's name — "when the parent item uses item options, this attribute is auto-generated, read-only"; live, a request sending "name": "cp0905-rev-var" read back as the six option-value names joined together. WooCommerce: the documented variation properties carry no name, yet the live response shows "name":"Red, No" — its two axis values. | 

 Member has its own short label, authored | 
 Akeneo · Salesforce B2C | 
 Akeneo ships a separate attribute for exactly this: the family variant puts variation_name at level 1, beside the model's own name. Salesforce: name is authored on the master and inherited, and a variant may define its own value. | 

 Full title repeated on every row | 
 Amazon · eBay · Walmart · Google | 
 These are the flat-row platforms, and they police the duplication. Amazon: the measured family carries a byte-identical title stem on parent and all three children. eBay: members' "product.title and product.description values … must have the same values". Walmart: both members of the sample carry the identical productName, differing only in flavor. | 

 What this means for us. Do not store the full title twice. The family holds the name; the member holds a short label that defaults to its axis values — "85 g", "Ayam Bawang" — and is only authored when the derived string reads badly. Compose for display, and index the composed string so staff can still search "Indomie Goreng 85 g". One place already needs the composed form and has the right instinct: the order line copies title onto itself at write time, which should become a snapshot of the composed string so a later rename cannot rewrite history.

## Images: I had this wrong

The first version of this page said the family owns the gallery and "a member may override with its own shot". That is not what the platforms do. They tie a picture to the axis value it illustrates — the red one, the 500 ml one — not to the member, and several of them will not let a member hold pictures at all.

How the picture is addressed | Platforms | What the source says | 

 Keyed to the axis value, on the family | 
 Salesforce · eBay · Tokopedia Era B · Akeneo | 
 Salesforce is the clearest: an image group carries "an ordered list of variation attribute values… If an image group applies for all the red variants, for example, it will have one of these elements, namely \"color=red\"", groups with no tag "represent the \"fallback\" images", and "the system chooses the most specific matching image groups possible". eBay: if aspectsImageVariesBy is set, "the links to the images must be passed in through this imageUrls container, and there should be a picture for each variation… if the variesBy.aspectsImageVariesBy field is set to Color, a link should be included to an image demonstrating each available color in the group". Tokopedia: "You can attach images to only 1 type of sales attribute… An image must be provided for each value of the primary attribute." Akeneo's worked example puts "the composition and the pictures" at the colour level. | 

 Member has no pictures at all | 
 Shopee | 
 Images are required on the item; the model has no image field. Instrumented: across all 92 API records, 1,704,281 bytes, model-scoped paths matching /image/i = 1, and that one belongs to the kit-item API, not to a variant. Option-scoped image paths in the same sweep = 11. The pictures hang off tier_variation.option_list[].image — the axis value again. | 

 One picture per member, drawn from the family's list | 
 Shopify | 
 "Each variant can have one media item applied, which must be from the product's media list", and "you can only add one image to each product variant". A live test assigning a foreign image succeeded and added it to this product's media list, so the subset invariant holds by adding rather than by rejecting. | 

 Member owns pictures, family is the fallback | 
 WooCommerce · Magento | 
 WooCommerce: the variation's get_image_id() falls back to parent_data['image_id'], the REST schema gives the variation a singular image against the parent's images array, and the storefront "swaps the product gallery to that variation's images. Clearing the variation selection brings back the parent product gallery." Magento decides it in three separate places, and the cart thumbnail ships defaulting to the parent: <configurable_product_image>parent</configurable_product_image>. | 

 Free-form on both, no relationship | 
 Square · commercetools | 
 Square: item and variation each have image_ids, and a live read-back had the item's images and one variation's images populated independently while the sibling variation had none. commercetools is the opposite extreme: there is no images field on the product at all, and "Images: images that are specific to a color and size combination" — every image action requires a variantId or sku. | 

 Repeated per row, because there is no family | 
 Amazon · Google · Walmart | 
 Google requires image_link on every variant row and has no family object. Amazon requires one on the parent too: "you must still describe other aspects of the parent product, including an image that represents the generalized product. The only information to omit relates specifically to price, availability, and shipping." Walmart has no family object on the write side at all: every image field sits on the item, and the group's picture is simply a nominated member — isPrimaryVariant, which "will appear as the image when customers search and the first image displayed on the item page. This should be set as \"Yes\" for only one item in a group of variants." It does key one thing to the axis, the swatch: "Enter the swatch image location in \"Swatch Image URL\" and its corresponding variant attribute name in \"Swatch Variant Attribute\". Required for products with visual variations, like color or pattern." | 

### Whether a member needs its own picture depends on the axis, not the member

Amazon publishes this as a table. Reproduced whole:

Dimension type | Description | Image behavior | Example | 

Visual | "Options where a significant visual difference is applied to the product variant" | "Images in the detail page will change depending on the option selected" | "Color or pattern variants" | 

Non-visual | "Options where there is no visual effect on the product regardless of selection" | "Images will remain the same" | "Size variants" | 

 What I recommended, and what was decided. I would have hung ProductImage off the family with a nullable pointer to the axis value it illustrates, resolving by most specific match — Salesforce's rule — with nothing on the member. The decision taken on 2026-09-15 was member-first with the family as fallback, which is WooCommerce's rule. Both work; the difference is that the axis-keyed shape stops a size-only family storing the same photograph once per member, and the chosen shape does not. Our data makes that cheap to live with either way: only 30,740 of 105,754 products carry any image at all.

 The counter-case, stated fairly. Square and commercetools give the member its own free-form list, and commercetools has no family images whatsoever. If we expect members to be photographed independently — different packaging shots per pack size, say — that is the model to copy instead, and the cost is that every member needs its own upload.

## What the group row gains that no product row has today

The dimension list — a thin through table of ProductAttribute rows with a position, each required to belong to the family's category and to be axis-eligible. Empty for a family of one.

The type — the Category that owns the attributes, and the gate on which of them may be an axis.

A variant key per member — a canonical string of the member's axis values, unique within the family, so D7's uniqueness is a database constraint instead of a validator over many rows.

A primary member — which member the card, the feed and the page default to. Walmart requires exactly one; Google picks the first; we will need one for the thumbnail and the default selection.

## The four things worth deciding deliberately

1 · Does the member keep a copy of main_category? A validated duplicate keeps age-walling (models.py:563), inventory replenishment, the search document and the feed reading the column they read today. Moving it wholly to the group re-points all four. The duplicate is the cheaper migration; the single copy is the cleaner model.

2 · How do non-axis attribute values attach to a family? ProductAttributeValue has one product column. Either add a nullable variant_group beside it with a check that exactly one is set, or give the family its own value table. The first keeps one read path for "all values of this thing"; the second keeps each table's constraint simple.

3 · What shape is the member's URL? The customer page is shop/product/{product_id}/{product_slug} today, and that exact string is what the Merchant feed sends Google. Google will not accept a feed whose variants share a link: "Make sure to have different landing page URLs submitted for each variant, with each URL using a different path segment and/or query parameters". So this is not family-or-member. The family owns the slug, and the member still needs an address — either a query parameter on the family page or the member id kept in the path. Keeping the id in the path is the only option that leaves every existing URL working.

4 · Is the title composed or stored? "Indomie Goreng" + "85 g" can be composed at read time, as Shopify does with displayName, or stored per member as it is today. Composition removes a drift pair; storage keeps the order line, the label and the POS display reading one field. The survey leans to composing: the only platforms that store the full string on every row are the ones with no container, and all of them have to police the duplication.

## One sentence that summarises the whole split

 Money, stock, identity, visibility and pictures stay on the member; words and classification move to the family. Almost every relation pointing at Product today is transactional and does not move at all. After the 2026-09-15 decisions the list that moves is shorter still: the description, the slug, the type and the placement M2M. Images gain a family option but keep their member rows, and attribute values do not move at all until the attribute mechanism is decided. That is the whole migration surface on the content side, and it is the same list whichever of the two tables ends up being called Product.
