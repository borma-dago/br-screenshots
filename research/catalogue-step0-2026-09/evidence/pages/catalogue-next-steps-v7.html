Step 0 · Lock the questions before the answers

The D-numbers were written as if "attribute", "category" and "family" were already defined. They are not. Thirteen questions, one already closed. Green-edged cards are tier 1. Nothing here is answered; the small line under each card is what Sunday's research reads.

A · What is an attribute?

Six questions about the thing itself: what it is for, what it is, whether an axis is one, whether categories share it, what a value is, and which side of the family line a fact lives on.

A0 tier 1

What are attributes for, and are some of them hidden?

The uses on the table: the variant picker, search filters, a spec sheet on the product page, Google feed fields, and internal rules (age wall, replenishment, purchasing). Which do we want, in what order? And are some attributes customer-facing while others are staff-only?

  • Visibility is a property of the attribute definition: shown or hidden everywhere it appears.
  • Per category membership: Rasa shown for food, hidden for cleaning products.
  • Per channel: shown online, hidden at the till, sent to Google.
  • Each use pulls the design differently: filters want shared value rows (A4); the feed wants Google's names and a mapping; the picker wants an order; internal rules want a stable code that staff cannot rename away.

Touches A2, A4, D17. Sunday: Square's 10 visible + 10 hidden; Shopify metafield storefront visibility; eBay aspectUsage; Magento's is_visible_on_front / is_filterable / used_in_product_listing flags.

A1 tier 1

What is an attribute, and which facts are attributes at all?

Today title, price and barcode are columns; weight and manufacturer are attributes. What is the rule? And is a definition (name, type, allowed values, owner) a separate thing from a value (one product's answer)?

  • Universal and read by code → column; category-specific and staff-authored → attribute. Lock condition 2 already moves the four shipping dimensions to columns on this logic.
  • Everything is an attribute, even the title (Amazon).
  • Only a small fixed set of custom fields beside the columns (Square: 10 visible + 10 hidden).
  • Is brand an attribute, or an entity with its own table?

Touches D9, D18. Sunday: where each of the thirteen draws the column-versus-attribute line; Shopify keeps weight on the variant, outside the attribute system.

A2 tier 1

Is a variant dimension an attribute, or its own object?

Flavour distinguishes Indomie siblings. It is also a fact to display, to filter on, and to send to Google. One thing with several roles, or two things?

  • One object with a role flag (WooCommerce is_variation, eBay, Magento). Then a group's axes are simply some of its category's attributes, and a member's axis value is its ordinary attribute value. Nothing new to store.
  • Two objects: options for variation, attributes for everything else (Shopify, Square, Shopee). Then the group declares its axes, and a member stores a "selected option" separately from its attribute values.

This is D3, closed 2026-08-13 as (a) on a 4–4 tie and confirmed by Lock 1. Answer A1 first, then confirm or reopen it, and say which on the record.

Touches D3, D4, D5, D7, D8. Sunday: what each side pays — duplicated values, two editors, two filter paths.

A3 tier 1

Is "Rasa" one attribute type shared by every category that wants it, or does each category define its own?

Food has Rasa. Instant noodles also want Rasa. The same type, reused, or two separate ones? This is the attribute matrix: a category × attribute membership table, or attributes nested under categories.

  • One global type; categories subscribe to it (Shopify: 8,240 definitions, 93,007 category edges; Akeneo; Magento). One search filter across categories; values survive a category move; one list to curate.
  • Each category owns its own copy (eBay: 197,046 leaf-scoped aspects; the 2a designs as written). Each category curates its own; no cross-category filter; a move loses values.
  • Shared type, per-category value list: one Rasa, but noodle flavours and snack flavours are different lists.

Touches A4, D4, D15, D16, D17. Sunday: how many Shopify categories reuse Flavor; distinct names among eBay's aspects; the distinct attribute names our 639 main nodes would need.

A4 tier 1

What is a value, and where does its allowed list live?

Is "Soto" a string typed on each product, or a row that many products point at? Is "215 g" a number with a unit, or text? Who decides the order the picker shows?

  • A value is a free string · a shared option row · or hybrid by type (select-typed → row, free text → string).
  • The list lives on the attribute (one list everywhere) · the category (per-category list) · the family (Shopify's option values belong to the product).
  • Quantities are text · a number under a unit fixed on the definition · a number plus a unit on the value (Akeneo's metric type).

Subsumes D8, D9. Today: 0.7% case drift, 25–32% junk in manufacturer, '0' on 11,071 rows, 28,750 titles carrying a unit token. Sunday: Shopify linked versus unlinked values; Square shared option values; Akeneo option order and metric type; GS1 and BPOM net-content rules.

A5 tier 2

Does an attribute belong to the product, to the product variant, or both?

"Product" here is the family row Lock 2 gives every product; "product variant" is the sellable member. Brand and description are facts of the product; flavour, barcode and price are facts of the variant. Is each attribute pinned to one side, can it live on either, and who says?

  • Pinned on the definition: each attribute is product-level or variant-level, fixed (commercetools level).
  • Either side, chosen per family: a family decides which of its attributes vary (Akeneo's distribution; Shopify metafields exist on both Product and ProductVariant).
  • Always on the variant; the product carries nothing, and shared facts are copied across siblings.

If an attribute is on the product, its variants do not store it. An axis is variant-level by definition (A2). Subsumes D11; touches D10.

B · What is a category node?

Four questions about the tree, plus one already closed: one category per product, as Amazon has one product type.

B1 tier 1

Do nodes have types — is a leaf a different kind of thing from an internal node?

Is Snack a folder that only groups, or a type that can own a schema and hold products?

  • Two kinds: folders and types.
  • One kind; "leaf" only means no children today (Shopify).
  • One kind with a per-node switch, so the rule can tighten later without a migration (Akeneo; lock condition 5).

Today: 1,553 leaves, 339 internal nodes, 292 of them holding products. Sunday: eBay's leaf-only rule (error 62009) and what sellers do about it.

B2 tier 1

Can a product be assigned to a non-leaf node?

19,459 products (18%) sit on non-leaf nodes today, 1,722 of them on a root.

  • Yes, any node (eight of thirteen platforms).
  • Leaves only (eBay, Walmart, Shopee, Tokopedia), so 19,459 products need a home before Lock 1 ships.
  • Per node: an "accepts products" switch.

Sunday: which of our 292 used non-leaf nodes are real types (a product genuinely belongs at "Snack") and which are catch-alls (Mainan dan Alat Lainnya: 1,048 products at depth 1).

B3 tier 1

How deep should leaves go?

For Snack, do we go on to potato snacks? What earns a node its existence: a distinct attribute set, a browse need, a reporting need, a minimum product count?

  • Today: depth up to 8; 607 used nodes hold fewer than ten products; 114 nodes cover half the catalogue; 639 cover 90%.

Equals D16; sizes Lock 1's authoring bill. Sunday: Shopify's depth and attributes per node in the food branch; Amazon's 107 food types against Google's 5,595 nodes; how many of our leaves share an identical attribute need.

B4 tier 1

What flows down from parent to child, per kind of fact?

If Food has Rasa, does Instant noodle get it automatically? Same question for requiredness, allowed values, age-walling, replenishment settings, "accepts products".

  • Nothing flows; every node is complete on its own (nine of thirteen).
  • Inherit with override (Salesforce: sub-category over parent over global).
  • Universal set on the root, additions on the leaves, nothing in between.
  • Probably per kind of fact, not one switch: attributes may inherit while age-walling must.

Equals D15. Sunday: eBay's category-features "inheritance override model"; the #11188 survey of 2026-09-15 (one of thirteen inherits down a category tree).

One category per product.

A product has exactly one category, as Amazon has exactly one product type. Closes the first half of D14. Where a merchandising node such as "Promo Ramadan" lives moves to step 5; promo already targets ProductRange, so nothing waits on it.

C · What makes products a family?

Three questions about the group row that Lock 2 gives every product.

C1 tier 2

What may two siblings differ in, and what must they share?

Differ only in axis values, plus each one's price, stock and barcode? Or also in title, images, description? Must they share the category? The brand? Can one product be in two families?

Why: under Lock 2 every product has a group, so this defines "sibling" for 105,000 rows and is the rule the merge and split screens enforce. Sunday: Google and Meta "consistent content across variants" rules; which fields actually differ across the 33 Indomie rows.

C2 tier 2

Is a 6-pack, or a bundle, a variant of the single — or a separate product?

#11126 ruled a warehouse-banded pack is its own SKU. D6 named "pack form" as an axis. Is "sachet versus bottle" the same idea as "1 versus 6"?

  • Count is an axis: the 6-pack is a sibling of the single.
  • Count is a separate product; form is an axis (Amazon: package_level is a mechanism apart from variation).
  • Both are separate products, no family link.

Touches D6, #11126, promo bundles. Sunday: Walmart count and multipack fields; GS1's GTIN-per-pack rule; count our own "x6" / "isi 6" titles.

C3 tier 3

Can a family have sub-families?

Shirt → colour → size, two levels; or one level only.

Equals D12. Apparel today: 240 of 1,945 products carry a size token. Sunday: #10943; Akeneo's two levels.

The order, and why it is this way

Each step needs the one below it to exist. The concepts say what the words mean; the registry says which attributes a category has; a value is what an attribute holds; an axis is an attribute whose value differs between siblings; a group is what siblings share. Nothing in steps 1 to 4 can be designed without step 0.

The value D8 · D9

What a value is. Framed by A4 above. Adds one storage question moved out of step 0: rows or a document? Today every value is one ProductAttributeValue row per (product, attribute) with six typed columns, 469,179 rows; the alternative is a JSONB document per product (Akeneo moved from rows to raw_values JSONB in 3.0), or rows plus a JSONB projection for reads. A foreign key from a value to an option row exists only with rows. The only part with measurable pain today: case drift on 0.7% of values and junk on 25 to 32% of manufacturer rows. An option type must exist before any axis does, because "Ayam Bawang" cannot be anything but free text in today's six value columns.

Implementation details naming · feed key · collapse totals · D14

No model consequence. Decide these when the migration is written. Naming (L2.3): keep Product as the sellable row and name the container; renaming Product to ProductVariant churns every FK, serializer and TS model for nothing. Feed key: an opaque stable item_group_id decoupled from the group PK, because Google treats it as immutable. Step 1 starts with one fork

Surfaced on #11188. Everything in the registry hangs on it, and it decides how heavy L1 turns out to be. Nothing is recommended here yet; this is the first decision to take.

Keep authoring our own tree

Needs
Inheritance down the tree with override (D15 = b), so 1,892 nodes do not need 1,892 schemas. The only fully worked reference is eBay's category features, delivered as an "inheritance override model" that integrators are told to store and manage client side.
Precedent
One of thirteen inherits attribute definitions down a category tree (Salesforce B2C, single route). Akeneo inherits down its Family tree, a different object, paid edition only.
Bill
Staff-authored schemas on a depth-8 tree. 114 nodes cover 50% of products, 398 cover 80%, 639 cover 90%; 607 used nodes hold fewer than ten products each. 46 catch-all nodes hold 3,766 products.
Fits
L1 as written, and the tree's existing business logic (age-walling, replenishment).

Adopt the Shopify Standard Product Taxonomy

What it is
MIT-licensed, 14,606 categories, 8,240 attribute definitions with closed value lists (only 1.0% of nodes have none, median 6), materialised per node with no inheritance. id-ID is a published locale: categories, attribute names and values all translated.
Example
Makanan, Minuman & Tembakau > Item Makanan > Produk Susu > Yoghurt carries Rasa (30 values), Kandungan lemak (8), dietary preferences including Halal, storage requirements, allergen information.
Gaps
No net-content or size attribute: 300 of 8,240 definitions match size/volume/weight and every one is category-specific. Our size axis is not solved by adopting it. The id-ID build has 18 duplicate full_name paths (Tableware and Flatware both render as Peralatan Makan).
Bill
Re-homing 105,771 products onto a foreign 14,606-node tree, which is the re-labelling cost L1 was chosen to avoid, though it is per category, not per product, and machine work once a mapping exists.

The same fork settles the non-leaf question. Products on non-leaf nodes are not automatically illegal: eight of thirteen platforms permit it. Akeneo's model, leaf-only as a per-tree setting, is what lock condition 5 already asks for, so the rule can tighten later without a migration.

A sequencing tension in the lock, resolved by this order

Lock condition 1 says build the category attribute schema editor first; condition 3 says ship L2 before L1. The editor belongs to L1 and cannot be designed until step 1 is decided.

  • Read together they mean: decide the registry (step 1) → build the editor → decide value and axis (steps 2, 3) → ship L2 with groups that can have axes → decide the group row's content (step 4) → only then the L1 authoring at scale.
  • So L1's schema change is small and early (ProductAttribute.category replaces product_class, needed by the editor), while L1's authoring bill is late and sized by step 1.
