🔒 Locked 2026-09-16
The category is the product type · every product belongs to a group
One object, not two: the Category tree owns the attribute definitions and the axis eligibility, and ProductClass retires. The variant group is mandatory — a separate table, a NOT NULL key from the sellable row, and a family of one for the products that will never have a sibling. Whether the two tables end up named Product → ProductVariant or ProductGroup → Product stays open.

Taken after four schema designs, an adversarial review, and four system passes across py/mono/solvent and ts/. The full record — what was rejected and why, what each lock costs, the five conditions it was taken on, and what would reopen it — is #10966 comment 5693781761.

Order of work: the category attribute schema editor first (it does not exist in any form today and blocks every shape), then the four shipping dimensions out of the attribute system, then lock 2 before lock 1 — the group delivers a capability on day one, the merge is a day-one no-op.

What this means for our three open pieces

Our questionWhat the survey changes
L2.1 · Do attributes inherit down our tree? The industry answer is no, and the reason is worth noticing: the platforms that materialise per node either author the taxonomy centrally (Shopify, Amazon, Walmart, Shopee) or have a flat type registry that is not a tree at all (commercetools, Magento, Walmart). Neither of those describes a 1,892-node tree that our own staff author by hand. So the real choice is not "inherit or not" — it is adopt a centrally-authored taxonomy and materialise per node, or keep authoring our own tree and build inheritance to make it affordable. That ties item 5 directly to item 3.
The 19,459 products on non-leaf nodes Not automatically illegal. Amazon, Shopify, Google, Magento, WooCommerce, Square, commercetools and Salesforce all permit it; eBay, Walmart, Shopee and Tokopedia forbid it. The most useful model is Akeneo's: leaf-only is a per-tree setting rather than a law, so the rule can be tightened later without a migration.
If we do build inheritance, what is the reference? eBay's, which is the only fully worked one: "Each child category in the tree inherits its settings from its parent category, so the defaults set at the root node flow down through the rest of the tree… there are some categories that 'override' the root settings". It is delivered as an inheritance override model, and eBay tells integrators to "Store the data locally and then manage the data on the client side" — i.e. even the platform that inherits does not compute the effective set on every read.
Route health, stated because two of these rows carry weight. Salesforce's ancestor-category walk rests on one route, a single generated doc set. Every Akeneo Family-tree fact comes from help.akeneo.com only and the feature is absent from the open-source edition. Google's "requiredness reaches descendants" also has one route. The three questions, highest level first

D1, D2, D3 and D13 were closed on 2026-08-13 against four platforms. The evidence beneath them is now thirteen, and the reading changed. Nothing here reopens a decision; it lays the thirteen out so each can be confirmed or re-made deliberately.

Q1

Is the ProductType the same object as the Category?

D1 · D2 — closed 2026-08-13

Two different things get called "category": the object that decides which attributes a product has (the type) and the object a customer browses to. One row, or two?

✅ Locked 2026-09-09 — one object. The product type is the category; D2 stands on the thirteen-platform evidence. Reasons given: (1) a separate category object means re-homing every product — too much work for the gain; (2) a separate browse/merchandising object can be added later. (2) confirmed: a browse-only secondary category, collection or tag object is additive — a new object and one M2M edge, no schema touched, no product re-homed; the dead ProductCategory table is already that edge, and Shopify, eBay, Square and Salesforce all bolt browsing on this way. The only thing that is not easy later is moving schema ownership off this tree onto a new type object, which is the option rejected here and never needs to happen.   • One page per family for free. Amazon: children are "grouped together by a parent listings item to provide a single detail page experience." S SP-API workflows guide. Adobe: "A configurable product displays as a single product with dropdown options for variations (like color or size)." S

Reading (mine, not evidence)

Two questions separate the three shapes. **Is the family row a Product?** A says yes: shared content and a page come free, and the cost is a non-sellable row among sellable ones — an invariant on twelve consumers and a nullable barcode. Common to all three designs
  • **The product type is the Category node** (Q1 locked: one object). It is the thing that decides what a family may vary on, in three steps that are the same in all three designs: (i) ProductAttribute.category replaces product_class — the type owns the attribute definitions (D1 + D2; whether a node also offers its ancestors' attributes is D15); (ii) the type marks which of its attributes may be an axis — ProductAttribute.is_axis_eligible (D4's gate; eBay's aspectEnabledForVariations and Akeneo's type whitelist are the precedents; the alternative is to declare no gate and let any attribute of the type be an axis, as Square and WooCommerce do); (iii) the family picks its dimensions from that eligible set (D5, the eleven-of-thirteen shape). Optionally the type can go one step further and declare named dimension sets a family must choose whole — Amazon's variation_theme — as a ProductTypeVariationTheme table; not sketched here because D5 is open.
  • The chain is therefore: Category (type) → owns ProductAttribute rows → some flagged axis-eligible → the family's dimension through-table may only reference those → each member stores a value for each dimension. The type constrains the family at step (ii) and (iii); it never stores the axis itself.
  • An option attribute type is needed before any axis exists: today's six value columns cannot hold "Ayam Bawang" as anything but free text (D8).
  • Dimension selection is a family-level list of ProductAttribute rows, each required to belong to the family's category and to be axis-eligible there (steps ii–iii above).
  • What a dimension row is. ProductVariantDimension / ProductVariantGroupDimension is a real table but a thin one: family FK, attribute FK, position. It marks the role and owns nothing else — the name is the attribute's, the allowed values are the attribute's option list, whether the list is closed or free text is the attribute's setting (and under D1 + D2 grocery Ukuran and apparel Ukuran are two attributes owned by two categories, so no per-family flag is needed), each member's value is its ProductAttributeValue row, and eligibility is the attribute's is_axis_eligible. Precedent: Magento's catalog_product_super_attribute (product id, attribute id, position, per-store label); Amazon is thinner still (a theme string on the parent); Shopify and Square made the option a full object with its own values, the D3 alternative not taken. The row can grow a display-label override or a picker default later without breaking D3.
  • Axis storage is the member's own ProductAttributeValue rows for the dimension attributes — D3 taken literally, one table for axis and non-axis values alike.
  • **variant_key**: a denormalised canonical string of the member's dimension values (for example flavour=soto;size=85g), unique per family, so D7's uniqueness is a database constraint rather than a validator over many rows. Akeneo stringifies for the same reason; Magento keys the pair.
  • Type equality: every member's main_category equals the family's category — a validator (or derived at save) in all three shapes; Amazon's parent is the same product type as its children, Magento's container and child share attribute_set_id.
  • Members keep upc exactly as today.

What the exercise adds to the pros and cons
  • The three designs share almost everything. The type's side — Category owning ProductAttribute, the is_axis_eligible gate, the rule that a family's dimensions come from its category's eligible attributes — is identical, as are the dimension table, variant_key, the type-equality rule and the option attribute type. A differs from B and C in whether the family row is a Product; B differs from C in one nullable flag on one foreign key.
  • A's invariant surface is a list, not a number: twelve sellable-only consumers plus the two online pointers on Product plus the search key. Each is a one-line rule; the risk is the one that is forgotten.
  • Promo does not need the family row in any shape. PromoProductLine targets ProductRange, an M2M of products; the family row is only a convenient way to fill a range.
  • **The Merchant feed keys on product.id, not upc,** so item_group_id is a one-field addition in every shape: A skips parents, B suppresses singletons, C needs neither.
  • **The search index is the one consumer that reads upc as an identifier** (guid). A must change that; B and C do not.
  • Replication is the default content policy of B and C, and it is the same policy the marketplaces enforce — and the same policy that produced today's drifting Indomie rows. The tooling to keep members aligned is the real cost, not the table.
