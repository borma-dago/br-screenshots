Six questions about the thing itself. Irvan, 2026-09-18: “before any of these, the biggest question is the ATTRIBUTE.” Brief: ATTR-DEF (A1 A2 A0) · ATTR-VALUE (A3 A4) · FAMILY (A5).

A1tier 1touches D9 · D18undecided

What is an attribute, and which facts are attributes at all?

Today title, price and barcode are columns; weight and manufacturer are attributes. What is the rule? Is a definition (name, type, allowed values, owner) a separate thing from a value (one product's answer)? Is brand an attribute or an entity?

Options

  • Universal and read by code → column; category-specific and staff-authored → attribute recommended
  • Everything is an attribute, even the title (Amazon)
  • Only a small fixed set of custom fields beside the columns (Square: 10 visible + 10 hidden)
  • Added by the brief, with reason: one definition registry over both storages — a storage discriminator says column or value row (Magento backend_type='static') take with option 1

Who uses which

9 of 13 draw the same line8 of 13 definition ≠ value

The 3 that do not are a PIM (Akeneo), an EAV monolith (Magento) and a feed (Google). Shopify states the payoff of a separate definition in one sentence: a defined metafield gets a typed editor, validation on write, search and filtering; an undefined one is a plain string merchants cannot search or validate.

Recommendation high · option 1 medium-high · option 4 medium · brand

Option 1, made decidable: a fact is a column when a code path reads it or a DB constraint must hold over it; an attribute when only humans read it and applicability depends on the category. Plus option 4: every product fact gets a ProductAttribute row (name, unit, order, visibility) and storage says where the value lives — so lock condition 2 moves the four shipping-dimension values to columns while the four definitions stay. Brand: an attribute with option rows now; its own table only when it needs a page, logo or navigation (the trigger three vendors state); never derived from manufacturer — 69.6 % of those are PT … companies, 3.2 % match the title brand.

Four repairs to ship with the schema editor: UNIQUE on the code within its owner; category non-null once Lock 1 lands; the display name actually served by the API; a typed unit on the definition (today the unit lives in a free-text name and a frontend i18n bundle).

What would reopen it

(i) Two or more staff-authored attributes acquiring code readers within a year — then promotion migrations cost more than option 2's read indirection. (ii) Lock 1 producing per-category counts large enough to need a governance cap — option 3 returns as a per-category cap. (iii) A second language or market, which makes the missing locale/scope columns urgent regardless.

Forces

Condition 2 confirmed as the first move, re-expressed (values move, definitions stay; 68.4 % of products carry 0 in those columns). D18: closed above the DB for free text, the FK is the enforcement for option-typed.

Is a variant dimension an attribute, or its own object?

Flavour distinguishes Indomie siblings; it is also a fact to display, filter on, and send to Google. One thing with several roles, or two things? D3 was closed 2026-08-13 as (a) on a 4–4 tie and confirmed by Lock 1 — the brief had to say on the record whether it confirms or reopens.

Options

  • One object with a role flag (WooCommerce is_variation, eBay, Magento): a group's axes are some of its category's attributes; a member's axis value is its ordinary attribute value recommended
  • Two objects: options for variation, attributes for everything else (Shopify, Square, Shopee): the group declares its axes; a member stores a “selected option” separately

Who uses which

declaration: 4 marked-attribute · 4 own-object · 1 neither · 4 unassignablevalue storage: 6–4 same store

The tie on declaration survives re-derivation — but it is on the wrong half of the question. On value storage the evidence leans 6–4 to the same store, and storage is the half that prices our build. Counter-evidence kept on the record: Salesforce migrated 1 → 2 and says why — value lists on a shared definition; that is A3/A4's to answer by scoping the list.

Recommendation medium-high

D3 CONFIRMED, not reopened, on the storage argument: an axis is a category-owned ProductAttribute marked eligible plus a thin per-family dimension row (group, attribute, position); a member's axis value is its ordinary ProductAttributeValue. One write path, one editor, one filter path instead of two. What carries it is our own code and Lock 1 (which gives axis eligibility a home only under one object).

#10778: V1 re-expressed (shared = reusable across categories via A3's mechanism) · V2 re-expressed (the governance flag becomes the input type) · V4/V5 confirmed in substance, home moved to the value · C5 referred to A3.

What would reopen it

Forces

What is a value, and where does its allowed list live?

Is “Soto” a string typed on each product, or a row that many products point at? Is “215 g” a number with a unit, or text? Who decides the order the picker shows? Today: 0.7 % case drift, 25–32 % junk in manufacturer, 0 on 11,071 rows, 28,750 titles carrying a unit token.

Options

  • Value: a free string · a shared option row · hybrid by type (select-typed → row, free text → string) hybrid recommended
  • List lives on: the attribute (one list everywhere) · the category (per-category list) · the family (Shopify's option values belong to the product) attribute recommended
  • Quantities: text · a number under a unit fixed on the definition · a number plus a unit on the value (Akeneo metric) label + derived magnitude recommended

Who uses which

13 of 13 store the value the same way at their own levelordering: 5 hand-order a shared list · 3 generate from the label · 0 from a stored quantity

No disagreement with the matrix's D8 tally. Shopify linked vs unlinked values, Square shared option values, Akeneo option order and metric type, GS1 and BPOM net-content rules were all collected for this card.

Recommendation medium-high · (c)+(A) medium-high · derived magnitude high · strings alone insufficient

(c) hybrid by type, list on the attribute, label + derived magnitude. Honest bill: one table, one column, one enum entry, one validator, one type-alias member, one API field, one form type. Normalisation alone reaches 1.6 % of the manufacturer mess; the junk is 31 distinct letterless values over 15,516 rows, deletable in 31 operations.

The V4-vs-matrix conflict resolved: both are true — V4 describes how a picker can be ordered, the matrix what a label does by itself. Neither priced the alternative: hand-ordering costs 886 labels (399 inside real size families) plus renumbering on insert; a derived magnitude costs 0 and is correct on the 782 of 1,993 size families that text order gets wrong. The typed-quantity capability has no owning issue today (#10942 is closed and never carried it).

What would reopen it

(i) A decision that staff must never type a free value — then pure rows beat hybrid and manufacturer must be converted before anything ships. (ii) Evidence that a derived parse is wrong on real staff labels — tested on 886 title-derived labels only; a sample of 200 staff-entered values (1 LUSIN, 1/2 KG) would settle it. (iii) A5 putting values at the family level in a shape that cannot carry an FK. (iv) The fork being taken, which pre-decides this anyway.

Forces

D8: rows, not JSONB. D9: label + derived magnitude; net content as an axis: yes. D7: implementable in the DB — lock condition 4's composite-FK mirror becomes a real constraint. C8 (one Ukuran, never split) confirmed and strengthened.

4–3 shared, among the unambiguous6–3 counting hedged rows

(a′) one global attribute registry + a CategoryProductAttribute membership row carrying required / display_order / optional name_override; a second type (not a subsetted list) when two categories need different vocabularies. #10778 C5 stands as re-expressed: global registry + per-category membership table — three briefs converge on it.

(ii) A decision that two categories must not share values — a merchandising reason we do not have today. (iii) The staff editor proving unable to express membership (lock condition 1's own risk). What are attributes for, and are some of them hidden?

Uses on the table: the variant picker, search filters, a spec sheet on the product page, Google feed fields, internal rules (age wall, replenishment, purchasing). Which do we want, in what order? Are some attributes customer-facing and others staff-only?

Options — where visibility lives

  • On the attribute definition: shown or hidden everywhere it appears recommended, as purpose flags
  • Per category membership: Rasa shown for food, hidden for cleaning products
  • Per channel: shown online, hidden at the till, sent to Google

Who uses which

10 of 13 on the definition5 of those with a named qualification1 of 13 per channel

Square's 10 visible + 10 hidden, Shopify metafield storefront visibility (a five-valued enum reduced to two writable values in three years), eBay aspectUsage, Magento's is_visible_on_front / is_filterable / used_in_product_listing all collected.

Recommendation high · on the definition medium · flag set

Uses ranked: 1 spec sheet (live and broken today) › 2 internal rules (read columns; keep it so) › 3 variant picker › 4 Google feed (sends nothing today; costs the method plus a ProductAttributeValue receiver plus a save-ordering fix) › 5 search filters (need A4's rows; the index carries no attribute field).

Visibility as purpose flags, not one boolean: is_customer_visible, is_filterable, display_order — the last replaces both the staff-form priority array (product-attribute-ordering.service.ts:9-15) and the backend alphabetical sort on the customer spec sheet (serializers.py:219-221). (i) A till/POS product page that must show a different attribute set from the web — then per-channel becomes real and Shopify's access triple is the cheapest precedent. (ii) A decision to send product_detail rows to Google — then a per-attribute “export” flag, kept separate from customer visibility (Walmart's per-feed-type vocabulary).

Forces

The schema editor's field list (condition 1): code unique + immutable, name, type, required, is_axis_eligible, is_customer_visible, is_filterable, display_order, unit, level, storage. Decision-free defect: Product.save() enqueues the index and the Google push at models.py:489 before attribute values are written at :490.

Does an attribute belong to the product (family row), to the product variant (member), or both?

Brand and description are facts of the family; flavour, barcode and price are facts of the member. Is each attribute pinned to one side, can it live on either, and who says? An axis is member-level by definition (A2).

Options

  • Pinned on the definition: each attribute is family-level or member-level, fixed (commercetools level) recommended · A5-a
  • Either side, chosen per family (Akeneo's distribution; Shopify metafields on both Product and ProductVariant — card correction: Shopify's MetafieldDefinition.ownerType is one value, so it is not a per-family example)
  • Always on the variant; the family carries nothing, shared facts are copied across siblings

Who uses which

4 declared on the definition2 fixed by structure1 per family4 absent (no family content row)1 read-time fallbackeBay ambiguous

A split with no plurality once “pinned” is split by mechanism. Level and visibility are independent fields of the same definition (commercetools, Shopify); “family-level = visible” has no precedent.

Recommendation moderate-high

A5-a: ProductAttribute.level (family | member, default member) + a nullable variant_group_id beside ProductAttributeValue.product with CHECK (num_nonnulls(…) = 1) and two partial uniques. No SameForAll flag (family level is it); the read-time fallback kept for images only. Day-one no-op: level defaults member, all 471,143 rows untouched; the 2026-09-15 tentative decision (values on the member) survives.

What would reopen it

(1) A1's rule producing attributes whose level genuinely differs family by family within one category — then A5-b becomes necessary and A5-a a lie told by a default. (2) The staff editor cannot express two levels — A5-c is the honest retreat, at the measured 18.9 % drift.

Forces

D11: partition by level, each fact stored once. D17: requiredness per level on the membership row. D10 (with C1): description, title, slug, main_category → family; images member-first; never price/stock/barcode/visibility — plus a work item nobody had named: a family-row reindex receiver and a family-image trigger (every reindex today is post_save on Product).

B4tier 1= D15undecided

What flows down from parent to child, per kind of fact?

If Food has Rasa, does Instant noodle get it automatically? Same question for requiredness, allowed values, age-walling, replenishment settings, “accepts products”. Irvan named this one on 2026-09-18 (“D15 is great as a concept”).

Options

  • Nothing flows; every node is complete on its own
  • Inherit with override (Salesforce: sub-category over parent over global) recommended for definitions
  • Universal set on the root, additions on the leaves, nothing in between
  • Per kind of fact, not one switch recommended

Who uses which

like-for-like: 1 of 2 inheritsSalesforce inherits · Shopify does not (authors 14,606 nodes centrally)

The honest denominator is platforms whose internal nodes own a schema, so inheriting is a real choice. eBay inherits the fact it attaches tree-wide (features) and not the leaf-only one (aspects) — direct evidence for per kind. Our own code already carries six kinds of category-derived fact resolved by four mechanisms.

Recommendation high · per kind medium-high · (b) for definitions

D15 is (d) per kind of fact. Definitions: (b) inherit, add-and-override, never remove. Requiredness inherits with the definition, a child may tighten. Allowed values inherit, a child may narrow (A4 owns the model). Age-walling must flow by ancestry — today's exact-id list is a live bug. Replenishment and is_public already flow. accepts_products must not flow.

What would reopen it

If the effective-set computation measures badly on the staff product list or the reindex (the eBay caution), (b) becomes “materialise into a join table maintained by a signal” — (a) with a generator, same semantics. If the fork goes to adoption, (b) is unnecessary and D15 collapses to (a).

Forces

The schema editor must show inherited vs own rows from day one. The category move needs a pre-flight and a subtree reindex, neither of which exists (#10919's shape). B3 and B4 are one decision.

What may two siblings differ in, and what must they share?

Differ only in axis values, plus each one's price, stock and barcode? Or also in title, images, description? Must they share the category? The brand? Can one product be in two families?

  • (i) Siblings may differ in anything; the family is a label
  • (ii) Family-owned content, member-owned commerce recommended

8 of 13 siblings must not differ in title, description or category3 police it on flat rows · 5 own it on the family12 of 13 forbid one product in two families

Barcode identity (the kept Sunday measurement): upc is unique by constraint, so “duplicates” is zero by construction. Provenance is the real measure: 57 % of all products carry a server-generated 987 code, but that is an offline-stock artefact — on the active-online surface 90.6 % carry a real GS1 GTIN. Can a family have sub-families?

Shirt → colour → size, two levels; or one level only. Apparel today: 240 of 1,945 products carry a size token.

Options

  • (b) Two levels, capped (Akeneo; Salesforce VariationGroup, Beta)
  • (c) Arbitrary nesting

Who uses which

9 of 13 one level2 nest, both capped at exactly two
