Prep findings · the short version
  • Online is small and concentrated. 1,904 orders from 608 customers in Jun–Aug 2026. 61 of those customers bring 57% of online revenue.
  • Margins are thin. The online gross margin is about 8.2%, or Rp16k on the median order. The plan's example Rp20k welcome voucher costs more than a median order earns.
  • The B2C margin assumption doesn't hold online yet. Online, household earns less than groceries (6.7% vs 8.7%), and fashion has almost no online sales.
  • B2B customers can't be identified anywhere in the system or the warehouse.
  • SEO and email both need engineering first. Google can't read the site without running its JavaScript, and there is no sitemap. No consent or unsubscribe exists for email or WhatsApp.

What the plan leaves open

Added by Claude · not in the PDF Claude's reading of the plan

These are gaps in the document, listed so they can be settled early. They are not criticisms of the approach.

  • No budget. Ads are tested on "a small testing budget each", with no Rupiah figure for ads, tools or incentives.
  • No numeric targets per channel. The only number is a Google rating of at least 4.5. The closing section names repeat purchase, customer lifetime value, cohort revenue and word of mouth as targets, without values.
  • No owners or timeline. The plan does not say who runs each channel or in what order the channels start, beyond "Search ads first" and the five email steps.
  • The calendar starts now. The promo calendar opens with 10.10 and payday in October 2026. 10.10 is on 10 Oct, eleven days from 29 Sep.
  • Consent before sending. Email and WhatsApp may only go to people who opted in (UU PDP). No customer has a consent record today, so the plan has no starting list until consent is collected.

The offer

From the PDF violet notes are additions

Content brings people in. The offer is what makes them buy and come back, so the plan builds it first.

Borma for Business (B2B)

It targets three problems business owners have: time lost shopping, stock arriving late, and juggling too many suppliers.

  • One order for all stock: staples, food ingredients, spices, beverages, cleaning supplies, packaging.
  • Scheduled delivery around Dago, for example every [day] and [day].
  • Fast reorder: a saved regular shopping list, confirmed in the app or through a dedicated business admin.
  • Guarantee: missing or damaged items replaced in the next delivery, subject to the complaint SOP.
  • Clean invoices for business bookkeeping.
  • Competitive pricing for business-volume purchases.

B2B testimonials

  • Who: business clients with a record of repeat orders. Repeat orders are the strongest proof the service works.
  • Formats: WhatsApp screenshots with phone numbers blurred; reels and stories shot at the customer's business; the customer's own story tagging @bormadago, reposted to a "Testimoni" highlight.

  • Consent: ask before posting a name, face, business name or chat.
  • Reuse the best ones in testimonial ads and on the B2B page.

Make 10 versions with different hooks, scripts and footage. Ad testing: test, pick, scale

Platform and deliverability

  • Sender: our own domain, e.g. promo@bormadago.com or halo@bormadago.com, never a Gmail address.
  • Platform: needs automations, tags and a connection to bormadago.com order data. Options: Mailchimp, Brevo, MailerLite, Klaviyo.
  • Authentication: SPF, DKIM and DMARC on bormadago.com (start DMARC at p=none). Gmail and Yahoo also require one-click unsubscribe for bulk senders.
  • Consent: only people who signed up or ticked consent (UU PDP). An unsubscribe link in every email.
  • Warm-up: start with the most active customers, then grow volume gradually.
  • List hygiene: people who never open drag every email into spam, including for loyal customers. Run re-engagement, then remove anyone who still doesn't open or click.
  • Monthly review: open rate, click rate, orders, revenue per email, unsubscribes per segment.

D. Google Maps & Business Profile

From the PDF violet notes are additions

Reviews come first. They are what customers see before deciding to shop, and they strongly affect local ranking. Target: a rating of at least 4.5, starting with branches that have fewer than 100 reviews.

Reviews

  • Protect quality: reply to every negative review with the follow-up action management took. Add customer satisfaction to every employee's KPIs (smile, greeting, attentiveness, speed, customer focus) if not already there.
  • Grow volume: Google Review QR code at the cashier; cashiers invite a review after each transaction; ask B2B clients after their first smooth delivery; invite customers to mention what they bought and the service (e.g. fresh vegetables, fast delivery).
  • Never: write reviews for customers, post from our own or bulk accounts, or give incentives for Google reviews. Google removes them and can suspend the profile.

Profile and ranking

  • Setup: claim the profile at business.google.com; match the categories top competitors use; set the location precisely; list the delivery areas; link bormadago.com and the B2B page; opening hours, photos and regular promo posts.
  • Ranking factors: distance to the searcher, fresh in-store photos, rating and review wording, NAP consistency, and other sites embedding or linking our map.
  • NAP: write name, address and phone identically everywhere. Find the listings of a strong (not the nearest) competitor by searching their address and phone, then register Borma Dago on the same sites.
  • Photos: taken in store with location on; add a geotag to older ones (geoimgr.com).
  • Map references: embed the map on bormadago.com; create a public Google My Maps map titled with the target keyword (e.g. "Supermarket & Supplier Horeca di Dago Bandung"). My Maps pins only show on that custom map.

Key reminder

From the PDF violet notes are additions

Ads and content can bring a customer in once. Only a good experience makes them come back and bring others. Protect three things: product quality (fresh, complete, as described), service (fast, friendly, accurate, complaints handled well) and competitive pricing.

The targets that matter most: repeat purchase, customer lifetime value, cohort revenue (each month's new customers keep buying afterwards), and word of mouth.
